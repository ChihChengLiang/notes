# MEV and Flashbot timeline


:::{timeline}
:reverse: false

2019 — MEV coined
: Daian, Goldfeder, Kell, Li, Zhao, Bentov, Breidenbach & Juels publish "Flash Boys 2.0" on arXiv, later at IEEE S&P 2020.[@flashboys2] Defines **miner extractable value (MEV)** and documents "time-bandit attacks" — retroactive chain reorgs incentivized by past extractable value — as a consensus-stability threat. Several co-authors later split: Daian co-founds Flashbots; Juels and Breidenbach build Chainlink's rival Fair Sequencing Services.

September 2020 — Chainlink Fair Sequencing Services proposed
: Juels, Breidenbach & Tramèr present Fair Sequencing Services at ETHGlobal's MEV.WTF Summit and in the Chainlink 2.0 whitepaper.[@fss] A decentralized-oracle transaction-ordering design aimed at *minimizing* MEV rather than productizing its extraction — the direct rival philosophy to Flashbots.

November 23, 2020 — Flashbots founding manifesto
: Alex Obadia publishes "Flashbots — Frontrunning the MEV Crisis" on Medium.[@frontrunning] Frames MEV as posing "negative externalities and existential risks" to smart-contract blockchains and proposes not elimination but a "permissionless, transparent, and fair ecosystem for MEV extraction," organized around three pillars: **Illuminate, Democratize, Distribute**.

March 2021 — MEV-geth adoption "hockey-sticks"
: Flashbots' March 2021 Transparency Report notes adoption "went hockey-stick" as miners turned to MEV as a substitute for mining income ahead of EIP-1559.[@transparency] By February 2022, independent measurement finds effectively all prominent Ethereum miners enrolled — MEV-geth had become "a de facto part of the Ethereum protocol."[@flashinpan]

April 7, 2021 — "Front-Running-as-a-Service Is Theft"
: Ari Juels, Ittay Eyal & Mahimna Kelkar publish a CoinDesk op-ed arguing that systematizing MEV extraction as a service amounts to theft from users — a direct rebuke of the Flashbots approach, thanking Phil Daian for comments on the draft.[@theft]

July 19, 2021 — Flashbots' internal ethical reckoning
: Alejo Salles & Kristof Gazso publish "MEV and EIP-1559" on writings.flashbots.net.[@mev1559] Admits that supplying MEV-geth to ~85% of hashrate "amounts to Flashbots having established an effective off-chain miner coordination mechanism, which naturally invites an exploration of its potentially nefarious implications." Concludes EIP-1559 did little to change MEV dynamics.

November 9, 2021 — Daian's "inevitability" defense
: In a Decential Media interview, Flashbots co-founder Phil Daian responds to the "theft" critique: "MEV is in many ways inevitable... Ultimately, MEV is generated by the users, not us."[@decential]

November 2021 — MEV-Boost proposed for the Merge
: Flashbots publishes "MEV-Boost: Merge-ready Flashbots Architecture," the proof-of-stake successor to MEV-geth and the practical embodiment of **Proposer-Builder Separation (PBS)**.[@mevboost]

August–September 2022 — The Merge and the OFAC censorship spike
: Following the Merge, MEV-Boost adoption reaches ~50% of validators within a month and ~90% within two.[@adoption] After OFAC sanctions Tornado Cash, Flashbots' relay filters sanctioned transactions; OFAC-compliant blocks rise from ~9% to over 50% within a month, peaking at 79% on November 21, 2022.[@ofac]

October 2022 — "Almost too successful"
: Phil Daian's Devcon VI talk, adapted as "MEV for the Next Trillion" on writings.flashbots.net, concedes: "We have been very successful in our mission to democratize MEV extraction, almost too successful... We run centralized infrastructure today: our MEV-boost Relay and our block builder."[@nexttrillion]

November 23, 2022 — SUAVE announced
: "The Future of MEV is SUAVE" proposes a decentralized, MEV-aware, privacy-first encrypted mempool and block builder explicitly designed to "eliminate any central points of control, including Flashbots."[@suave]

February 15–16, 2023 — MEV-Share introduced
: Robert Miller introduces MEV-Share, an "orderflow auction" letting users and wallets capture MEV their own transactions create via a new class of "matchmakers."[@mevshare]

2023 — MEV burn proposed
: Justin Drake publishes "MEV burn — a simple design" on ethresear.ch, extending EIP-1559's burn logic to MEV by requiring builders to burn a subjective base fee, pitched publicly as "EIP-1559 but for MEV."[@mevburn]

October 2023 – March 2024 — Builder concentration measured
: Öz, Sui, Thiery & Matthes ("Who Wins Ethereum Block Building Auctions and Why?", AFT 2024) find that three builders — beaverbuild, rsync, and Titan — produced roughly 80% of MEV-Boost blocks in this window, out of the ~90% of all Ethereum blocks produced via MEV-Boost.[@builderconcentration]

June 2024 — EIP-7732 (enshrined PBS) authored
: Francesco D'Amato, Barnabé Monnot, Michael Neuder, Potuz, Justin Traglia, Terence Tsao & Nico Flaig author EIP-7732, moving PBS into Ethereum's consensus layer to remove trusted relays and MEV-Boost middleware entirely.[@eip7732]

November–December 2024 — BuilderNet launches
: Flashbots, Beaverbuild, and Nethermind jointly launch BuilderNet, a decentralized, TEE-based block-building network. Flashbots migrates all its orderflow to it and ceases operating any centralized block builders on Ethereum, stating: "Two parties build 90% of blocks on Ethereum today. This centralization... directly threatens Ethereum's resilience and neutrality."[@buildernet]

## How did the M word evolve


:::{timeline}
:reverse: false

2014 — Early prediction of miner front-running
: Pseudonymous trader "Pmcgoohan" predicts on a Bitcoin/Ethereum forum that miners could exploit their view of pending transactions and control over ordering to front-run trades, asking "what is to stop front running by a miner in any marketplace implementation?"[@pmcgoohan] — years before the phenomenon is named.

2019 — "Miner Extractable Value" coined
: Daian, Goldfeder, Kell, Li, Zhao, Bentov, Breidenbach & Juels publish "Flash Boys 2.0" on arXiv, defining MEV as "the total amount of ETH miners can extract from manipulation of transactions within a given timeframe" — explicitly scoped to miners in a proof-of-work context.[@flashboys2]

November 2020 — Flashbots founding usage
: Flashbots' founding manifesto and early GitHub materials use "Miner Extractable Value" throughout, consistent with the original PoW framing and Ethereum's mining-based consensus at the time.[@frontrunning]

2021 — Flashbots docs relabel preemptively
: Flashbots' own documentation begins explicitly noting the shift: "MEV stands for Maximal Extractable Value (previously defined as Miner Extractable Value)." The same FAQ observes MEV "will still exist in the future... after Ethereum shifts to 2.0" — relabeling in anticipation of the coming proof-of-stake transition rather than waiting for it.[@flashbotsdocs]

Late 2021 — "Maximal" becomes Flashbots' working title
: Flashbots' own "MEV in 2021: A Year In Review" event recap opens by calling it "Maximal Extractable Value (MEV)" outright, treating the renamed term as already settled a full year before the Merge.[@yearinreview]

September 2022 — The Merge cements the rename
: Ethereum's transition to proof-of-stake replaces miners with validators, making "miner" technically inaccurate. Ethereum.org's explainer states plainly that "since the transition to proof-of-stake via The Merge validators have been responsible for these roles, and mining is no longer part of the Ethereum protocol. The value extraction methods still exist, though, so the term 'Maximal extractable value' is now used instead."[@ethereumorg]

2022 onward — Multi-chain rationale reinforces the rename
: Chainlink's explainer gives the complementary driver: the term outgrew Ethereum-specific mining. "MEV is increasingly referred to as maximal extractable value instead of the original term miner extractable value. This is due to MEV not being limited to just miners in proof-of-work (PoW) blockchains, but also applying to validators in proof-of-stake (PoS) and other types of networks as well."[@chainlink]
